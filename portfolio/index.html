IBM Data Analyst Capstone: Tech Trends & Skills Analysis

Problem

Survey and job-market data are fragmented, which makes it hard to see which technologies developers use today, which they want to adopt, and what drives compensation.

Solution

Cleaned 11,552 survey responses to 11,398 unique records, combined them with 27,005 job postings, wrote 10 analytical SQL queries (CTEs, window functions) on SQLite, and built a five-tab Streamlit dashboard.

Result

JavaScript (76.2%) and SQL (62.3%) were the most-used languages, PostgreSQL the most-desired database (38.0%), and Python’s desired use was 15.3% higher than its current use. IQR analysis flagged 879 high-earner compensation outliers.

California House Price Predictor

Problem

House values depend on non-linear interactions between location, income and housing features, which a simple linear model captures poorly.

Solution

Engineered ratio features, compared HistGradientBoosting, Random Forest and a Linear Regression baseline on 20,640 records, and deployed the best pipeline as a Streamlit app with an interactive California map.

Result

HistGradientBoosting reached a test R² of 0.84 (RMSE ≈ $46k, MAE ≈ $31k), ahead of Random Forest (0.81) and the linear baseline (0.60).

🔬 MSC DISSERTATION (IN PROGRESS) • HEALTHCARE ML

Cross-Dataset Evaluation of ML Models for Heart Disease Prediction

MSc Data Science • Chandigarh University • Supervisor: Dr. Naveen Kumar Mani

Problem

Heart-disease models often look strong when tested on the same cohort they were trained on, but their performance and risk estimates can degrade at a different hospital with different patients and protocols.

Solution

Harmonised four public UCI cohorts (Cleveland, Hungarian, Zurich, VA Long Beach; 918 patients after removing two exact duplicate records from the 920 raw records) to 10 shared non-invasive features, trained Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM and XGBoost, and ran 24 cross-dataset transfer experiments with bootstrap confidence intervals.

Result

A strong ROC-AUC on one cohort did not guarantee reliable risk estimates on another. The study quantifies how discrimination, calibration (Brier score, ECE) and SHAP explanations change under dataset shift.

Core Methodological Contributions:
  1. Generalisation: 24 directional train-on-one, test-on-another experiments with 1,000-iteration bootstrap confidence intervals.
  2. Calibration: Brier score, expected calibration error, calibration slope and intercept, before and after Platt scaling.
  3. Explanation Stability & Shift: SHAP rank correlation and top-5 overlap across cohorts, alongside KS, Wasserstein and PSI dataset-shift measures.

🔬 MSc Dissertation • In Progress

Cross-Dataset Evaluation of Machine Learning Models for Heart Disease Prediction: Generalization, Calibration, and Explainability

Raghavendra Golla • Supervisor: Dr. Naveen Kumar Mani • MSc Data Science, Chandigarh University

📄 Abstract

Machine learning models for coronary artery disease often report strong discrimination under single-cohort validation, but performance can degrade when they are transferred to a different clinical setting. This study benchmarks Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM and XGBoost across four public UCI heart-disease cohorts (Cleveland, Hungarian, Zurich and VA Long Beach; 918 patients after removing two exact duplicate records) harmonised to ten shared non-invasive features. It measures how discrimination (ROC-AUC, PR-AUC), probability calibration (Brier score, expected calibration error, before and after Platt scaling) and SHAP feature attributions change under cross-cohort transfer and dataset shift. The work is in progress and has not yet been peer-reviewed; it is a research study, not a clinical tool.

Key Contributions

  • External validation: 24 directional cross-cohort transfer experiments with 1,000-iteration bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • Calibration: Brier score, expected calibration error, calibration intercept and slope, before and after Platt scaling.
  • Explanation stability: SHAP rank correlation and top-5 overlap across cohorts, related to KS, Wasserstein and PSI shift measures.
